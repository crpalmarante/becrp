# RFC-8001 - Chart of Accounts

| Field | Value |
|--------|-------|
| RFC | 8001 |
| Name | Chart of Accounts |
| Category | Accounting Core |
| Status | Draft |
| Version | 1.0 |

---

## Platform Philosophy

> **Simple is always better than complex.**
> Simples é sempre melhor do que complexo.

Design systems that are easy to understand, easy to maintain, easy to extend, and easy to use.
Avoid unnecessary abstractions. Prefer explicit behavior over hidden magic.
Every feature should solve a real business problem.
Performance and maintainability always come first.

---

# 1. Objective

This RFC defines the Chart of Accounts structure within the Accounting Core domain.

The Chart of Accounts provides the official structure for classifying accounting transactions across the platform.

---

# 2. Motivation

Every accounting transaction requires classification.

Examples:

- assets;
- liabilities;
- equity;
- revenue;
- expenses;
- costs.

A centralized Chart of Accounts ensures consistency across all business domains.

---

# 3. Responsibilities

Chart of Accounts is responsible for:

- defining accounting accounts;
- organizing account hierarchy;
- classifying account types;
- controlling account availability;
- supporting multiple companies;
- maintaining account history.

---

# 4. Non Responsibilities

Chart of Accounts does not:

- create journal entries;
- post transactions;
- calculate taxes;
- determine operational events;
- manage inventory valuation;
- manage financial payments.

These responsibilities belong to other Accounting Core components.

---

# 5. Account Structure

The Chart of Accounts follows a hierarchical model.

Example:

```text
1 - Assets

    1.1 - Current Assets

        1.1.01 - Cash

        1.1.02 - Bank Accounts

        1.1.03 - Inventory

2 - Liabilities

    2.1 - Suppliers

3 - Equity

4 - Revenue

5 - Expenses
```

---

# 6. Account Types

Supported account classifications:

## Assets

Resources owned by the organization.

Examples:

- cash;
- inventory;
- equipment;
- receivables.

---

## Liabilities

Obligations.

Examples:

- suppliers;
- loans;
- taxes payable.

---

## Equity

Ownership value.

Examples:

- capital;
- retained earnings.

---

## Revenue

Income generated by operations.

Examples:

- product sales;
- services.

---

## Expenses

Operational costs.

Examples:

- salaries;
- utilities;
- administrative expenses.

---

# 7. Account Properties

Each account should contain:

- account code;
- account name;
- account type;
- parent account;
- company;
- status;
- effective dates.

---

# 8. Account Hierarchy

Accounts may contain child accounts.

Example:

```text
Inventory

    Inventory - Retail

    Inventory - Warehouse

    Inventory - Consignment
```

This allows detailed reporting while preserving accounting structure.

---

# 9. Multi-Company Support

The Chart of Accounts must support:

- shared templates;
- company-specific accounts;
- account mapping.

Example:

```text
Global Template

        ↓

Company Chart of Accounts
```

---

# 10. Account Lifecycle

Accounts follow:

```text
Created

↓

Active

↓

Restricted

↓

Archived
```

Historical accounts cannot be deleted if accounting entries exist.

---

# 11. Account Restrictions

The system may define restrictions:

Examples:

- cannot post directly;
- only accepts automatic entries;
- requires analytic dimension;
- requires cost center.

---

# 12. Integration With Other Domains

Other domains consume account mappings.

Examples:

Inventory:

```text
Stock Valuation

↓

Inventory Asset Account
```

Sales:

```text
Sale Event

↓

Revenue Account
```

Loan:

```text
Loan Event

↓

Receivable Account
```

The operational domains do not own the accounts.

---

# 13. Audit

Changes to the Chart of Accounts must record:

- user;
- date;
- previous value;
- new value;
- reason.

Accounting structures require full traceability.

---

# 14. Architecture Principles

Chart of Accounts follows RFC-8000 principles:

- Accounting owns accounting classification.
- Accounts are not created by operational modules.
- Historical accounting data cannot be rewritten.
- Structure must support growth.
- Simple is always better than complex.
